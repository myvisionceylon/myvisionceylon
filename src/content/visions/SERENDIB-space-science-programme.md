## the vision

SERENDIB's Space Science Programme develops Sri Lankan expertise through satellite-data research, small spacecraft, scientific instruments and participation in international missions.

It begins as a distributed programme involving universities, research organisations and engineering partners rather than requiring one large physical site. The work could include Earth observation, satellite communications, spacecraft instrumentation, orbital analysis and eventually planetary-science payloads.

## why here

This programme does not need a single remote site. Research can be distributed across universities and laboratories, with shared testing and engineering facilities at the SERENDIB Research & Technology Centre. Ground-station locations should be chosen according to the missions they support.

## pros

- Provides an achievable entry point into space science.
- Develops software, electronics, remote sensing and systems engineering.
- Supports applications such as agriculture, coastal monitoring, disaster response and environmental research.
- Can begin with modest projects and expand as partnerships grow.
- Builds experience before more ambitious missions are proposed.

## cons

- Spacecraft require testing, reliability engineering and launch access.
- Funding, specialised components and international partnerships may be difficult to secure.
- Projects can remain isolated demonstrations without a long-term roadmap.
- Operational satellites require continuing ground support and maintenance.

## how it could happen

1. Agree on a national space science roadmap and research priorities.
2. Begin with satellite data, small payloads and university projects.
3. Establish shared electronics, software and environmental testing capabilities.
4. Partner with established organisations for launch access and mission operations.
5. Progress towards more ambitious instruments and international exploration projects.

## open questions

- What scientific or national need should the first project address?
- Which universities and international partners could participate?
- What test equipment can be shared?
- How will small projects progress into reliable operational missions?
