## the vision

The Research & Technology Centre is the engineering backbone of SERENDIB. It develops and maintains scientific instruments, antenna subsystems, electronics, embedded software, data-processing systems and precision mechanical equipment.

Unlike the radio observatory, it does not need to be in the quietest or most remote location. It should be accessible to engineers, researchers, suppliers, universities, reliable utilities and transport.

## why here

**First regions to compare:** Dambulla–Habarana and the Polonnaruwa corridor, with a university-linked location as an alternative.

These inland areas may provide a more central base for a distributed eastern-interior research programme than a facility deep inside a remote forest. However, actual site selection must compare road access, fibre, power reliability, staff recruitment, land availability, environmental constraints and connections to universities.

Peradeniya should not be ruled out: a university-linked centre could benefit from existing academic infrastructure and skilled staff. The choice should be made on measured practical advantages rather than the assumption that a traditional campus is inherently worse.

## pros

- Provides shared engineering support for multiple projects.
- Develops local skills in electronics, instrumentation, software and scientific computing.
- Creates a base for training, prototyping and technology transfer.
- Can support external research and industry projects as well as SERENDIB.
- Can be developed before the more expensive observatories.

## cons

- Requires investment in laboratories, equipment, calibration and skilled personnel.
- Some specialised components will remain imported.
- Recruiting and retaining experts may be difficult.
- Without a defined programme, it could become a general-purpose campus without a clear focus.

## how it could happen

1. Identify the engineering needs of the first SERENDIB projects.
2. Compare Dambulla–Habarana, Polonnaruwa and university-linked options.
3. Start with electronics, software, prototyping and data systems.
4. Add specialised radio-frequency, precision-mechanical and scientific-instrumentation laboratories as demand grows.
5. Establish shared training and support arrangements with the observatories.

## open questions

- Which location offers the best balance of staff access, utilities and transport?
- Could an existing university or technical institution host the first phase?
- Which laboratories are essential from the start?
- How will the centre support its own operations over the long term?
