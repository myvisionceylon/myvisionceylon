## the vision

A specialised underground laboratory would allow Sri Lankan researchers to participate in particle physics and astroparticle research, potentially including neutrino detection and cosmic-ray studies.

Rather than attempting to excavate a very large vertical shaft, the concept is to investigate a suitable hill or mountain that could allow a horizontal access tunnel into stable rock. This only works if the rock provides enough effective shielding for the chosen detector.

## why here

**First region to screen:** the Monaragala–Bibile hinterland, alongside mapped rock outcrops in the wider eastern interior and possible sites near—but outside protected areas around—the Maduru Oya region.

Sri Lanka has extensive ancient crystalline bedrock, but the presence of crystalline rock alone does not prove a site is suitable. Hill geometry, rock cover, fractures, weathering, groundwater, stability, seismic conditions, access and environmental constraints must all be investigated.

Maduru Oya National Park and its reservoir catchments are important ecological areas and should not be treated as available building land.

## pros

- Builds local capability in particle physics, detector systems and scientific data analysis.
- Could participate in international experiments before building a major detector.
- A horizontal access tunnel may be more practical than a deep vertical shaft where geology permits.
- Could share computing, workshops and engineering expertise with SERENDIB.

## cons

- A small residual hill may not provide enough overburden for a major neutrino detector.
- Excavation, ventilation, groundwater management and underground safety can be expensive.
- Scientific returns depend on detector design and a clear research question.
- The project may need to join an international collaboration rather than build a standalone facility.

## how it could happen

1. Define the intended detector and required overburden before selecting a hill.
2. Use GSMB geological maps to shortlist rock formations, then commission detailed mapping and geotechnical fieldwork.
3. Model three-dimensional rock cover and investigate groundwater, fractures, seismic conditions and access.
4. Begin with university research and participation in existing experiments.
5. Consider excavation only after the geology, environmental case, detector design and funding are proven.

## open questions

- Is the intended goal neutrino detection, cosmic-ray research or a smaller underground laboratory?
- How much effective rock cover does the selected detector require?
- Does any candidate hill provide that cover in the necessary directions?
- Could Sri Lanka make a stronger contribution by joining an established international experiment first?
