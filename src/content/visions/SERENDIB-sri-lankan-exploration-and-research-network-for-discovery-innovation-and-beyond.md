## the vision

SERENDIB is a proposed Sri Lankan scientific discovery initiative: a long-term network of facilities, researchers and partnerships exploring the universe and developing advanced research technologies.

It brings together six connected visions: a giant radio telescope, a deep-space communications station, a particle physics programme, a space science programme, a Research & Technology Centre, and the coordination needed to make them work together.

SERENDIB would not require every facility to occupy one campus. The scientific instruments should be placed where their individual requirements are best met, while connected by secure fibre networks, shared training, computing, engineering and collaboration.

## why here

- Sri Lanka has a varied landscape, ancient crystalline bedrock in many regions, a dry-zone interior and established universities and engineering talent.
- The eastern interior is worth screening for remote scientific facilities, but no candidate is yet confirmed as radio-quiet, geologically suitable or available.
- The national programme can combine distributed sites with a more accessible engineering and research hub.
- International partnerships could help develop specialist expertise and make the facilities useful to real research programmes.

## candidate location strategy

- **ACCRT:** Screen inland eastern dry-zone locations outside protected areas and dense settlements; test radio-frequency interference before selecting a site.
- **Particle physics:** Investigate suitable rock formations in the Monaragala–Bibile hinterland and other mapped crystalline-rock areas. Do not assume Maduru Oya's hills provide sufficient overburden.
- **Deep-space communications:** Screen inland Trincomalee and other eastern-interior sites against spacecraft visibility, interference and partner mission requirements.
- **Space science:** Operate as a national programme linked to universities, with no single site required initially.
- **Research & Technology Centre:** Compare an accessible site in the Dambulla–Habarana/Polonnaruwa corridor with a university-linked location, assessing staffing, utilities, transport and fibre.
- **Renewable energy:** Evaluate roofs and already-developed or low-impact land near the chosen research hub, using site-specific solar and grid studies.

## pros

- Creates a coherent long-term scientific vision.
- Builds links among astronomy, physics, engineering, computing and space science.
- Allows projects to advance at different speeds and in different locations.
- Encourages international partnerships, high-skilled employment and research education.
- Avoids forcing all facilities onto a single site that may not suit every instrument.

## cons

- Requires long-term funding, governance and clear scientific priorities.
- Different facilities have different environmental, geological and radio-frequency needs.
- Major observatories may be expensive and require international partners.
- A broad umbrella could become unfocused unless each project has measurable objectives.

## how it could happen

1. Establish a national feasibility programme and identify university and international partners.
2. Commission separate site screens for radio astronomy, underground research and spacecraft communications.
3. Start with research, small instruments, data analysis and engineering projects that can use existing facilities.
4. Build major facilities only after scientific demand, site suitability, environmental approval and funding are demonstrated.
5. Connect the projects through shared engineering, computing, training and fibre infrastructure.

## open questions

- Who would govern SERENDIB and how would it be funded?
- Which facility has the strongest scientific case for a first investment?
- Which international partners could use or co-develop the proposed facilities?
- How will protected habitats, communities and existing land uses be safeguarded?
- What milestones would demonstrate scientific and public value?

## research notes

- Sri Lanka's Geological Survey and Mines Bureau publishes national geological maps at 1:100,000 scale; these should guide geological screening.
- Census 2024 includes population and occupied-housing data at Grama Niladhari level, which is more useful for local site screening than district averages.
- The Maduru Oya National Park and reservoir catchments are important ecological constraints; the park itself should not be treated as candidate construction land.
- Candidate regions in this file are preliminary screening areas, not approved or surveyed sites.
