## the vision

A Sri Lankan ground station supporting selected lunar, planetary and other spacecraft missions. Precision antennas would track spacecraft and receive telemetry and scientific data; transmitting commands would require authorised mission agreements and compatible equipment.

The station would initially be an independent facility seeking a specific role with an international agency, university mission or commercial operator. Building an antenna would not automatically make it part of NASA's Deep Space Network.

## why here

**First region to screen:** inland Trincomalee and other eastern-interior sites, compared against the actual coverage needs of potential mission partners.

The deciding factors are spacecraft visibility, horizon obstruction, radio-frequency interference, reliable power, fibre connectivity, weather, secure operations and access for large equipment. Sri Lanka's longitude alone does not establish a coverage gap or commercial advantage.

## pros

- Could connect Sri Lanka to international spacecraft operations.
- Develops expertise in antenna control, timing, radio-frequency engineering and mission software.
- Could support contracted ground-station services if a real customer need exists.
- Creates opportunities for university and international collaboration.

## cons

- Large antennas, transmitters, receivers and precision systems are expensive.
- Established networks have substantial technical and operational advantages.
- Continuous operations need reliable power, connectivity and trained staff.
- Mission contracts and technical compatibility must be secured before major investment.

## how it could happen

1. Identify potential partner missions and model their visibility from Sri Lanka.
2. Assess candidate sites for horizon, interference, weather, power and fibre.
3. Develop a smaller tracking or communications test facility where legally and technically appropriate.
4. Secure a mission partner and service agreement.
5. Build a deep-space-capable station only if the coverage and business case are demonstrated.

## open questions

- Is there a genuine global coverage need that Sri Lanka can fill?
- Which mission partner would use the station?
- What antenna diameter and frequency bands would be required?
- Could the station share support services with SERENDIB without interfering with ACCRT?
